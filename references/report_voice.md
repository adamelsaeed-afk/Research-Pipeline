# English research report voice

Write for a management reader who needs the evidence and its limits. Keep formal,
direct English. Use concrete subjects and verbs. Name the entity, capability, figure,
date, and source rather than relying on vague attribution such as “experts say.”

- Describe observed facts and documented claims precisely. Distinguish a vendor's
  published capability from independently measured performance or adoption.
- Vary sentence length naturally, but make each paragraph carry one finding.
- Remove grand claims, promotional adjectives, stock transitions, empty scene
  setting, and repeated three-part lists that add no information.
- State uncertainty plainly: “The public documentation did not specify…” or
  “The cited sources disagree on…”. Do not convert absence of documentation into
  proof that a feature does not exist.
- Keep claims and their citations attached. Preserve figures, units, comparisons,
  caveats, and calculation assumptions while editing.
- Finish with a factual synthesis. Do not add advice, opportunities, calls to
  action, or process notes.

After the edit, compare the prose with the validated findings and sources. A style
pass cannot introduce a new claim or remove a material qualification.
